# Brag Plan: Satvic Studio

## What is this app?
Satvic Studio helps a person review a dubbed video they cannot understand by comparing the source and dubbed audio and pointing to moments that may need a fluent speaker's attention.

## The angle
The hard part is not making a dub; it is knowing where to check it when you do not speak the target language. Show one Hindi-to-Marathi review moving from two tracks, through direct audio comparison, to a timestamped finding. The product narrows the human review to a moment; it does not certify a translation.

## Hook (first 2-3 seconds)
“You can hear the dub. You can’t verify every word.” The Hindi source and Marathi dub appear as two real-looking track cards, with the gap between them as the question.

## Key moments (the middle)
- The upload panel shows the actual two product paths: generate a dub with Sarvam or upload a dub that already exists.
- Both tracks are compared as audio; transcript labels appear as supporting evidence, not as the verdict.
- A report finding lands with paired `Source 00:26` and `Dub 00:26` play controls.

## Outro / punchline
“AI points you to where to listen. A fluent speaker confirms.” Close on `Satvic Studio · Dub QA`.

## User flow worth showing
Choose a Hindi source and Marathi dub → let the system transcribe and compare both audio tracks → open a timestamped finding and play the matching moments.

- Interpretation: restrained interface motion, readable copy, no hype or fake pass score.

Satvic Studio helps you find where a dubbed video may need a closer listen—even when you don’t speak the target language. AI points to the moment; a fluent speaker confirms it.

## Audio direction
- Role: warm, polished bed with restrained UI sounds
- Music: `happy-beats-business-moves-vol-1-by-ende-dot-app.mp3` (Sascha Ende, CC BY 4.0)
- Music treatment: start gently at 0s, keep under the interface, fade at 19.5s; no voiceover.
- Music cue guidance: bundled cue preset at 120.19 BPM. Land the human-confirmation line near 17.02s; the paired finding reveal occurs earlier with its scene. Use the beat grid for small non-text accents, never for unreadably rapid copy.
- Audio-reactive treatment: subtle glow around the active comparison panel, following RMS softly; no waveform visualizer.
- SFX posture: sparse and quiet; one soft click on upload and one warm impact on the finding reveal.
- Audio-coupled moments: upload action and finding card arrival.
- Restraint rule: sounds should feel like the app responding, not a game or an alarm.

## Storyboard

### Scene 1 — The gap — 3.5s
Two language chips settle on the Satvic Studio canvas: `Hindi source` and `Marathi dub`. The line asks: `You can hear the dub. Can you verify every word?`
Sequential/interaction: the source chip arrives, then the target chip; hold the question long enough to read.
Audio intent: music begins softly; a quiet transition accent.
Audio-coupled idea: chips land on two nearby beats, with the text held steady.
Transition mood: soft split reveal → Scene 2

### Scene 2 — Start with the two tracks — 3.5s
Recreate the actual `Analyze a dub` panel with `Generate with Sarvam` and `I already have a dub`. The second choice resolves into `Hindi original` and `Marathi dub` upload rows.
Sequential/interaction: cursor selects the existing-dub path, then both upload rows appear. Fictional file names only.
Audio intent: quiet UI confirmation.
Audio-coupled idea: a soft click on the selection.
Transition mood: clean slide → Scene 3

### Scene 3 — Listen to both — 4.0s
Paired audio panels show source and dub play controls. A restrained connector emphasizes that the review compares both audio tracks; transcript lines appear below, visibly labeled `supporting evidence`.
Sequential/interaction: source and dub wave traces animate independently, then align beneath the comparison heading.
Audio intent: the music gains a little warmth, without alarm or suspense.
Audio-coupled idea: soft active-state glow breathes with the music.
Transition mood: focused push-in → Scene 4

### Scene 4 — Go to the moment — 5.0s
The real report-card language appears: `Automated finding` and `Possible omission to review`. Beside it are paired `Source 00:26 ▶` and `Dub 00:26 ▶` controls. Small label: `Illustrative report view`.
Sequential/interaction: finding card resolves first; the two time controls appear together and remain readable.
Audio intent: one warm, soft reveal accent; no success chime.
Audio-coupled idea: finding reveal near a strong music cue if timing remains readable.
Transition mood: steady crossfade → Scene 5

### Scene 5 — Human confirmation — 4.0s
The report card recedes into the dashboard. Final copy: `AI points you to where to listen.` then `A fluent speaker confirms.` Finish with `Satvic Studio · Dub QA`.
Sequential/interaction: two lines reveal one at a time with a comfortable hold.
Audio intent: music resolves gently and fades.
Audio-coupled idea: final brand lockup settles near a strong cue.
Transition mood: soft fade to finish

**Music mood for this video:** warm, polished, lightly upbeat.
**Audio summary:** a quiet bed supports the two-track comparison, one soft finding reveal, and a calm, responsible close.
